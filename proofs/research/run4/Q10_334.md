# The (3,3,4) and (2,4,4) parent cores

**PROVED, independently audited in [Q10_REVIEW](Q10_REVIEW.md) and
[Q10_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW](Q10_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md).** Each of these two previously
untreated cores gives an all-Z, multiplicity-one local 5↔5 relation among
its ten admissible source facets. Four walls in the (3,3,4) relation are
spurions. The other six, and all ten walls for (2,4,4), are BCFW tiles.
This is local parent geometry, not a complete-tiling or generation theorem.

The apparent asymmetry of (3,3,4) does not prevent a reflection: its two
three-element classes can be exchanged. This actual automorphism, with
its source/target orientation characters checked below, explains the
equal wall counts. No new Plucker identity is needed to force balance.

## 1. Complete source charts and all source boundaries

Rotate and if necessary reflect the cores to consecutive classes A,B,D,
with sizes (4,3,3) or (4,2,4), respectively. Thus A=(1,2,3,4), while
B=(5,6,7), D=(8,9,10), or B=(5,6), D=(7,8,9,10).
Normalize the three distinct ordered projective directions to horizontal,
diagonal and vertical:

\[
C(x)=\begin{pmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3&a_4&b_5&\cdots&b_{4+b}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&0&b_5&\cdots&b_{4+b}&d_{5+b}&\cdots&d_{10}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

All ten coefficients are positive, modulo a common positive scalar. A
projective transformation of a line is uniquely determined by the images
of three distinct points. This normalization therefore gives the entire
positive parent source cell, with no remaining proportionality equation,
cross-ratio, order inequality or disconnected source branch.

**PROVED / SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Its thirteen codimension-one source facets
are the ten single-column deletions and the three cyclic class mergers.
This follows from the complete rank-two source classification, checked
independently by basis inclusion and affine labels in the archived core
inventory. Every deletion wall is admissible: after deleting the zero
column it is a source-certified n9 cell. For (4,3,3), deleting an A-column
gives (3,3,3), one of the published spurion cells; deleting a B- or D-column
gives a general BCFW cell. For (4,2,4), every deletion gives a general BCFW
cell. Exact recipes/certificates and increasing support embeddings are
stored for each wall. Run3's full map transport makes these all-Z
certificates at n10, rather than numerical rank evidence.

Every merger has a parallel class whose complementary support has at most
four elements. Its image dimension is at most seven by the class-complement
Schubert bound. These three facets are retained as collapsed walls. They
are not secretly counted as coordinate deletions, and no full-dimensional
merger is omitted in these two profiles. The different (1,4,5)/(2,3,5)
cores need their separate merger analysis.

## 2. Full fibers and a positive global pivot

Fix any positive 10×6 external matrix Z. Put

\[
U=\sum_{i\in A}a_iZ_i,\quad V=\sum_{i\in B}b_iZ_i,
\quad W=\sum_{i\in D}d_iZ_i,\qquad Y=\operatorname{span}(U+V,V+W).
\]

For Y in the open parent image or any selected open wall image, U,V,W
are nonzero positive combinations of their indicated external rows.
Let E_i be the projection of Z_i into R6/Y. The complete image-containment
equations are the eight homogeneous linear equations with columns

\[
M_Y=[(E_i,0)_{i\in A},\ (E_i,E_i)_{i\in B},\ (0,E_i)_{i\in D}].
\]

For nonnegative rank-two C(x), positive Z preserves its rank, so row
containment is equality of the image plane. Together with the complete
source chart, this identifies the entire positive fiber with the
projectivization of ker(M_Y) intersected with the positive orthant.

**PROVED.** M_Y has rank eight throughout this locus. A dual dependence
is given by covectors alpha,beta annihilating Y, with alpha annihilating
Z_A, beta annihilating Z_D, and alpha+beta annihilating Z_B. Annihilating
the two rows of Y forces alpha(V)=alpha(W)=0. The six vectors
(Z1,Z2,Z3,Z4,V,W) are independent: their determinant is a strictly positive
sum of ordered external six-minors. Hence alpha=0. Then beta annihilates
the six distinct B∪D rows, so beta=0.

Choose the kernel pivot (b6,b7) for (4,3,3), and (b5,b6) for (4,2,4).
Write B_I=det(Y1,Y2,Z_I) for an ordered four-bracket. Deleting the two pivot
columns from M gives a block determinant, up to a common positive square
factor from the quotient-coordinate choice:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
(4,3,3):&D_{b6,b7}=B_{1234}B_{5,8,9,10}<0,\\
(4,2,4):&D_{b5,b6}=B_{1234}B_{7,8,9,10}>0.
\end{array}
\]

Indeed B1234=det(Z1,Z2,Z3,Z4,V,W)>0. In the first case
B5,8,9,10=det(U,V,Z5,Z8,Z9,Z10)<0, since the b6 and b7 terms each
move Z5 past one later B-row. At an open wall at most one of b6,b7 is
zero, so the negative sum stays strict. In the second case the bracket
is det(U,V,Z7,Z8,Z9,Z10)>0. These formulas prove the chosen pivot nonzero
for every positive Z and throughout all ten selected open walls.

Thus there is a globally smooth normalized kernel basis K_Y with pivot
rows equal to I2. The positive projective fiber is an interval: normalizing
the sum of the two pivot coordinates to one gives a bounded intersection
of linear inequalities in one variable. It contains a positive point
when Y is in the open parent image. There are no other positive branches.

## 3. Image-side coefficients and all-Z constancy

Let the pivot indices, counted from zero, be p<q. Orient the projective
fiber by T=K_Y(x_q,−x_p). For i<j the symbolic formula is

\[
\det(K_i,K_j)=(-1)^{i+j-p-q}\frac{D_{ij}}{D_{pq}}.
\]

Extend antisymmetrically when i>j. At an open coordinate wall x_i=0,
choose any j≠i; since x_j>0 the image-side sign is

\[
\epsilon_i=\operatorname{sgn}\det(K_i,K_j).
\]

This is the Run3 complementary-minor identity with the actual pivot
indices, rather than a guessed alternating source-boundary sign.
It is computable from the source pattern and positive Z. Wall admissibility
is an independent input: if the wall coordinate vanished identically on
the two-dimensional kernel, there would be a positive interval of
distinct preimages in that same source wall. Injectivity excludes this,
so the wall normal is everywhere nonzero.

The globally normalized pivot and positive source/external parameter
spaces are connected. Consequently each wall sign is constant over the
whole positive-Z family. Exact rational evaluations can now determine
these already constant signs; their all-Z validity does not follow from
samples alone. Ambient elimination in C(x)Z=HY, without a quotient basis,
independently verifies every evaluated coefficient.

The resulting vectors in source-coordinate order are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{profile}&A&B&D\\\hline
(4,3,3)&+,-,+,-&-,+,-&+,-,+\\
(4,2,4)&+,-,+,-&+,-&+,-,+,-.
\end{array}
\]

In particular the four spurion walls alternate +,−,+,−. The classwise
sums are (0,−1,+1) and (0,0,0), respectively. A separate structural reason
for zero total, independent of these rational evaluations, follows next.

## 4. The actual reflection and why it reverses fiber orientation

For (4,3,3), use the label reflection rho(i)=1+(4−i mod 10), so its list
of images is (4,3,2,1,10,9,8,7,6,5). It reverses A and exchanges B,D.
Let J be the signed column permutation sending old column i to rho(i),
with sign + on A and − on B∪D. Put

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then C(Px)=H C(x)J, where P is the ordinary permutation of the ten
positive coefficients by rho. There are five transpositions and no fixed
coordinate. The source projective nine-dimensional orientation is reversed.
Here J²=I. For any six selected rows of JZ, with r coming from its first
four rows, the sorting sign and row signs together are
(-1)^(15−r(6−r)+6−r)=−1. Thus all ordered maximal minors of JZ are negative.
Choose G=diag(−1,1,1,1,1,1) and set Z'=JZG. This is positive,
and

\[
C(Px)Z'=H C(x)ZG.
\]

For (4,2,4), ordinary reversal of all ten labels exchanges A,D and
reverses B. Use J equal to the ordinary reversal matrix and H the row
swap matrix. Again C(Px)=H C(x)J, P consists of five transpositions,
J²=I, and Z'=JZG is positive because reversal of six rows has sign −1.
The same intertwining identity holds.

The ambient G action preserves the orientation of Gr(2,6): its tangent
orientation character is sign(det G)^2=+1. The source action reverses
orientation. The full kernel chart gives local coordinates (Y,pivot ratio),
so the image map is a submersion. The exact tangent orientation sequence
therefore says that these symmetries reverse fiber orientation. Since all
wall signs are constant throughout their positive parameter spaces, each
reflected pair has opposite signs. This pairs the ten walls into five
opposite-sign pairs for every positive Z.

For (4,3,3), two pairs are spurion-spurion pairs within A, and three pairs
exchange the B and D walls. Thus the non-BCFW walls participate directly
in this sign-count theorem. Reflection is not merely an apparent symmetry
of a list: its positive source and external actions have been exhibited.

## 5. Complete endpoint accounting and geometric relation

The lower-dimensional exception analysis is essential. In the pivot-sum
normalization, rank-two coefficient points with two or more zero coordinates
belong to finitely many projective faces of dimension at most seven.
Their images have dimension at most seven. A rank-one coefficient matrix
with an active B pivot forces all A and D coefficients to vanish: a
nonzero diagonal column cannot be parallel to a nonzero horizontal or
vertical column. Its surviving image line lies in span(Z_B), of dimension
b≤3. Lines there have dimension b−1, and two-planes containing such a
line have dimension four. This exceptional incidence family has dimension
at most b+3≤6. Rank-one chart limits therefore hide no full-dimensional
endpoint. All merger facets have already been separately certified collapsed.

Generic Y in the open parent image consequently has exactly one open
coordinate wall at each interval endpoint, one with each orientation.
All ten wall maps are injective, so these are multiplicity-one endpoints.
Every selected open wall image belongs to the open parent image: its
nonzero inward fiber direction moves into the positive orthant. The
global pivot also proves openness of the parent image by continuity of
positive kernel coefficients as Y varies.

Each open wall image is open in Gr(2,6) by injectivity and invariance of
domain. An overlap of two same-side open wall images would be open and
would meet the generic locus, contradicting endpoint uniqueness. Thus
same-side open images are exactly disjoint. Source incidence puts every
closed selected tile in the closure of the parent image; generic endpoint
coverage and compact source closures give the reverse inclusion for each
finite side union. Both closed unions therefore equal the closed parent
image. This proves the ordinary all-Z local tile relation, modulo sets
of dimension less than eight, with equal closed covered regions.

The proof separates the interval relation from its reflection-based sign
count. It uses neither raw canonical-form equality nor complex degree,
and does not assert relation generation or that these replacements occur
inside any specified complete tiling.
