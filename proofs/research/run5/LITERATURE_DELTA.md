# Run 5 literature delta: the missing global bridge

Audit date and cutoff: **2026-09-06**. This is a bounded primary-source audit of generation, connectivity, and chain-complex routes. It supplements the earlier literature files; it does not replace their audit or claim priority from an unsuccessful search. New mathematical statements below are separated from source statements and from the preserved Run 4 theorem.

## Status after the audit

| Proposed bridge | Status established by this audit |
|---|---|
| Every all-Z rank-two m=4 tiling difference is generated by nine-dimensional source-parent relations | **CONJECTURAL** for general n. No theorem supplying this bridge was located. |
| Every such tiling is connected by admissible parent flips | **CONJECTURAL** in general; Galashin–Lam Conjecture 13.11 is the explicit published formulation. |
| The bridge for n=7 | **PROVED, source-derived special case:** parity to cyclic polytopes plus their flip connectivity. See [BAUES](BAUES.md). This is not a new infinite-family result. |
| Every distinct pair of tilings has a common positroid refinement in the source-Bruhat subdivision poset | **FAILED:** tilings are minimal in that poset. Arbitrary semialgebraic common partitions do exist, with different atoms. |
| Regular secondary-fan connectivity accounts for all tilings | **FAILED as an unconditional inference:** nonregular cyclic-polytope triangulations already exist. For m=4 beyond polytopes, the cited secondary constructions have additional scope and hypotheses. |
| Source cellular boundaries automatically yield admissible-tile indicator relations after deleting bad facets | **FAILED:** the two Run 4 hostile parents give a direct obstruction. See [CHAIN_COMPLEX_LITERATURE](CHAIN_COMPLEX_LITERATURE.md). |
| Every source cell closure is a ball, with a valid signed cellular differential | **SOURCE-VERIFIED:** Galashin–Karp–Lam regularity; Olson's orientation analysis. This is a statement about the source. |

Here “no theorem located” records the outcome of this search, not a proof that no theorem exists. The preserved [Run 4 theorem](../run4/LOCAL_PARENT_THEOREM.md) determines all constant-coefficient all-Z indicator relations supported on the admissible facets of **one** nine-dimensional parent. It does not establish either global bridge in the first two rows.

## Primary sources that resolve the main scope questions

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Galashin–Lam, *Parity duality for the amplituhedron*, Definition 7.1 and Theorem 7.2, treat source-indexed subdivisions and their all-Z parity transport. Section 13.4 separately states local equal-size boundary triangulations (Conjecture 13.10), flip connectivity (Conjecture 13.11), the four-mass warning (Example 13.12), Baues sphericity (Problem 13.13), and a regularity problem (Problem 13.14). None should be cited as a general generation theorem. [Author PDF](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~galashin/papers/duality.pdf), [publication/arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00600).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Mohammadi–Monin–Parisi, *Triangulations and Canonical Forms of Amplituhedra: a fiber-based approach beyond polytopes*, Section 5.2–5.3, develops the secondary-fan description in the conjugate-to-polytopes case n−k−1=m. The argument explicitly assumes Conjecture 5.6 and independence of the fan combinatorics from the image point. Remark 5.6 excludes nonregular dissections from the family produced by Theorem 5.11. Corollary 5.10 concerns a common refinement of **fiber normal fans**, not a common positroid refinement of arbitrary tile decompositions. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.07254).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Postnikov, *Positive Grassmannian and Polyhedral Subdivisions*, Section 8.2, distinguishes generalized Baues topology from connectivity of the rank-zero/rank-one flip graph. Its graph/subdivision correspondence concerns two-dimensional hypersimplex projections; Section 13 discusses prospective extensions. [Primary PDF, pp. 15–16 and 22–23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.05307). The positive tropical Grassmannian secondary-fan theorem of Łukowski–Parisi–Williams concerns regular positroid subdivisions of the hypersimplex and m=2, not arbitrary m=4 tilings. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06164).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Rambau proves cyclic-polytope flip connectivity; Rambau–Santos proves the corresponding Baues sphere result and explicitly treats alternating oriented-matroid realizations. The latter also records nonregular triangulations, including C(9,4). These sources give the scoped n=7 route and the regularity warning. [Rambau, Mathematika 44 (1997), 162–194](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300012055), [Rambau–Santos author PDF, Theorems 1.1–1.3 and pp. 1–3](https://personales.unican.es/santosf/Articulos/Old/bauescyclicI.pdf).

## 2025–2026 updates affecting the canonical-form route

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Brown–Dupont's *Positive geometries and canonical forms via mixed Hodge theory* constructs a linear canonical-form map from relative homology for genus-zero pairs, with compatibility under boundaries, residues, and suitable pushforwards. This supplies genuine machinery, but its hypotheses and its domain of classes must be checked before use on an amplituhedron. It provides no automatic unit value on every tile. [Primary PDF, introduction and Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03202).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED, new audit item.** Koefler–Pavlov–Sinn, *Positive Genus Pairs from Amplituhedra*, arXiv:2601.11142v2, 17 February 2026, Theorem 5.1, proves that for k≥3 and n≥4(4k−1), generic positive Z gives

\[
g\bigl(Gr(k,k+4),\partial_a\mathcal A_{n,k,4}(Z)\bigr)
\ge 1+\frac{3k-5}{2}\deg Gr(k,k+4)>0.
\]

This obstructs a blanket genus-zero argument in that ambient pair. **It does not cover k=2, and positive genus does not refute the original positive-geometry definition:** Section 6 explicitly demonstrates the distinction. [Primary PDF, Theorem 5.1, pp. 16–17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11142).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED, scope screen only.** Hollering–Pavlov–Pratt, *Log Canonical Models and Positive Geometries*, 30 July 2026, Theorem 3.2, studies canonical embeddings under genus-zero and log-canonical hypotheses; its applications include arrangements, cubic surfaces, and moduli spaces. It does not supply an ordinary-amplituhedron tile-relation theorem. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28368).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED, scope screen only.** Even-Zohar–Parisi–Sherman-Bennett–Tessler–Williams, *Plabic Tangles and Cluster Promotion Maps*, develops promotion maps and higher intersection numbers. Its four-mass discussion includes positivity phenomena for algebraic promotion branches. These are not a constant positive-preimage multiplicity theorem for our fixed-Z map; the parameter spaces and maps differ. The screened introduction and Section 10 supply no missing tiling-generation result. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02891).

## Focused n=8 prior-art comparison

**SOURCE-VERIFIED.** Bourjaily, *Efficient Tree-Amplitudes in N=4: Automatic BCFW Recursion in Mathematica* (2010), p. 3 footnote 2 and Section 3.1 p. 15, reports **74 linearly independent twenty-term BCFW formula vectors involving 176 Yangian invariants**. These are not new numbers from this campaign. Seventy-four independent formula vectors with common nonzero coefficient sum have a 73-dimensional difference span by elementary linear algebra. This is numerically consistent with Run 5's local relation rank 73. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2447).

**SOURCE-VERIFIED; wording discrepancy retained.** Arkani-Hamed–Bourjaily–Cachazo–Goncharov–Postnikov–Trnka, *Scattering Amplitudes and the Positive Grassmannian*, pp. 85–86, instead calls 74 the number of independent **identities** obtained by equating those formulae. The formula-rank/difference-rank distinction plausibly explains this mismatch, but that is an inference; this audit has not reproduced the old package data. Sections 11–12 describe residue-boundary generation; Tables 1–2 list 14 cyclic rank-two invariant types and 24 cyclic classes of nine-dimensional boundary generators. The displayed hostile-parent identity retains the four-mass contribution. [Primary PDF, pp. 85–93](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.5605).

The proposed complete Run 5 n=8 theorem has a different required upper bound: every all-Z admissible tile must occur in the certified 176-cell universe, and an exact matrix of open-neighborhood **indicator** witnesses must have rank 103. Together with the independently certified 73-dimensional all-Z parent span, that would identify the entire indicator kernel and imply cardinality twenty without enumerating tilings. Prior computation of the BCFW formula span alone does not establish that upper bound or completeness for arbitrary positroid tilings. The independent Run 5 membership review, not this literature audit, determines whether the proposed certificate establishes them.

Targeted searches for n=8 or eight-point amplituhedron Magic Number, arbitrary triangulations, 176 invariants/tiles, 103 indicator rank, and 73/74 relations found the primary antecedents above. No equivalent complete all-Z n=8 indicator-kernel theorem was located in this bounded search. This is not a novelty certificate.

## Search record and limits

The audit queried combinations of “amplituhedron”, “flip connectivity”, “Conjecture 13.11”, “Baues”, “secondary fan”, “regular triangulations”, “common refinement”, “source boundary”, “relative homology”, and “chain complex”, with 2025/2026 variants. It followed primary references to Rambau, Rambau–Santos, Postnikov, Galashin–Lam, Mohammadi–Monin–Parisi, Galashin–Karp–Lam, Olson, Brown–Dupont, and the two 2026 algebraic-geometry papers above. Author publication lists were used for discovery; aggregators and their open/conjectural labels were not used as mathematical evidence.

No source found in this bounded audit proves all-Z m=4 flip connectivity or generation by the preserved rank-two parent relations for general n. This conclusion is explicitly provisional. In particular, publication-index absence is not a novelty certificate, and the cutoff is the current date, not the end of September 2026.
